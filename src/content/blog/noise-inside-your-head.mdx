title: "Signal, noise, sleep: why a full head can't think"
date: "2022-05-15"
description: "Your head feeling full isn't a metaphor. It's a signal-to-noise problem, and sleep is the fix."
topic: "science"
---

Ever felt your head was so full you couldn't fit another thought in? Turns out that's not just a figure of speech.

**Every new thing you learn makes a synapse stronger.** A synapse is the connection between two nerve cells; stronger connection, easier recall. That's why revision works — you're not adding information, you're thickening the wire.

But wires can only get so thick. Across a waking day, synapses keep enlarging as you absorb everything around you, and they eventually saturate. Past that point, the brain gets bad at learning new things and worse at retrieving old ones. It's a *costly* state: stronger synapses draw more energy, respond less selectively to stimuli, and start to bleed into each other[[1]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3768102/).

Here's the clean way to see it. Think of the brain as a communication channel:

\[ SNR = 20 \log \frac{V_s}{V_n} \]

(Shannon–Hartley, the same formula that governs how much a Wi-Fi channel can carry.) `V_s` is the action potential of the signal across a synapse; `V_n` is the ambient firing of everything nearby. As you push more energy into the channel through the day, both the signal *and* the noise scale up. At some point the noise catches the signal, SNR collapses, and the channel — your head — feels full.

Sleep is how the brain resets the channel. While you're out, weaker synapses get tagged by a protein and pruned in a process called [synaptic homeostasis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627313011355). The important connections stay; the noise around them shrinks. You wake up with a quieter channel and room to learn again.

A few things fall out of this:

1. **Ten minutes helps.** Even a short nap measurably drops the noise floor. If you're stuck, don't push harder — step away.
2. **The information isn't deleted, just shrunk.** If it were deleted, you'd wake up remembering yesterday's meeting but not your friends. This is the [plasticity–stability dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability%E2%80%93plasticity_dilemma) — the brain has to keep old memories intact while making room for new ones.
3. **The rut is physical.** A creative block at 11pm isn't a character flaw. It's an SNR reading.

Stuck on a problem? Sleep on it. Your channel is saturated, and the fix isn't more thinking — it's less.
