I came across [Epsilab](https://epsilab.com/) and [Akhara](https://akhara.ai/) last week, and both got me thinking about the same thing: RL environments are quietly becoming the most interesting shape of company in 2026.

Here is the whole idea in plain words.

An **agent** is a thing that tries. An **environment** is a room that says "good" or "bad" after each try. A **probability tool** is what the agent uses to guess how good the next try will be, before it tries it.

That's it. If you have those three, you have an agent that learns.

Now the trick: you don't have to build all three. If you are making a chatbot agent, you can plug in a video model from one company, a voice model from another, and a **probability tool** that scores partial trajectories. The one thing you still need is a room — an environment — that can actually run what the agent proposes and score it. That room is the product.

This is why the 2026 crop looks small and sharp. Epsilab and Akhara are not trying to be the agent. They are trying to be the room. A five-person team can build one good room. A frontier lab cannot easily build a thousand rooms, so they buy the rooms — or the scored data that falls out of them.

**The GTM is almost embarrassingly simple: get on [Pavlov's List](https://pavlovs.info/).** Pavlov's List is the running index of RL-environment companies that the big labs watch. Most startups that land on it get inbound from OpenAI or one of its peers within a few weeks. This is not a hack; the labs have run out of internet to train on, and RL is where the next jumps come from. Being on the list is a legible signal that your room is real, scored, and reproducible.

So the whole recipe for a 2026 environment startup fits on one hand:

1. Pick a domain where "good" and "bad" can be written in code.
2. Rent the video, voice, and vision models. Don't build them.
3. Log every rollout. The dataset is the moat, not the sandbox.
4. Publish a leaderboard.
5. Wait for the email.

The old wave tried to be the agent. This wave is trying to be the gym. It's a much smaller thing to build, and — as Feynman would probably point out — that's usually a sign it's the right thing.
